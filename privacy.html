Privacy Policy

TimeLocked · Last updated: 25 November 2026

TimeLocked (“the app”, “we”) puts a wait between you and something you want to keep away from yourself — a password, a code, a message, a purchase. It protects you from your own impulse, not from someone else who can unlock your phone. This policy says exactly what the app stores, where, and what it sends.

What is encrypted, and what is not

  • Encrypted on your device before it leaves it: the content of each entry (the text you lock, a password, a code) and the text of a letter to your future self. They are sealed with AES-256-GCM using a key that never leaves your Apple devices in readable form. We have no server and cannot read them.
  • Not encrypted: an entry’s name, and the details the app needs to run the wait and show your statistics — category, lock length and unlock times, a price you entered or the app read from a link, your “how strong is the urge” ratings, your decision and how it turned out, and dates. These are stored in plain form in your own private iCloud (see below), visible to anyone who can unlock your phone, and shown in the app’s list. Don’t put a secret in an entry’s name.
  • Who it protects against: the wait cannot be shortened by changing your device clock. But anyone who can unlock your iPhone can open the app, read entry names, open anything whose wait has already ended, and erase the vault. TimeLocked is a delay, not a safe.

Where your data lives

  • On your device. Entries are stored locally. Photos you lock and voice notes to your future self are not synced to iCloud by the app. They are included in your device backup (iCloud Backup or a computer backup) and come back if you restore from it; otherwise they are lost if you delete the app or change phones. Locked photos are encrypted by the app, apart from a preview at most 48 pixels wide or tall, which, like voice notes, is protected only by iOS file encryption.
  • In your private iCloud (if sync is on). Entries sync through your own private iCloud database using Apple’s CloudKit — encrypted content plus the plain details listed above. We never receive them. The encryption key is kept in iCloud Keychain so a new device can open your entries. iCloud is also used to get Apple’s server time, which is what makes the wait impossible to skip; without iCloud you cannot open an entry. When you delete an entry, it waits in Recently Deleted for 30 days; its record in your iCloud is marked as deleted but kept whole, so you can bring it back. When the 30 days run out, or you erase it yourself, its name, content and letter are blanked in iCloud; some plain details (such as dates, category and price) may remain in the erased record. Apple’s Privacy Policy governs iCloud.
  • No recovery. There is no account and no backup on our side. If you lose access to iCloud Keychain, or forget an optional master password, entries cannot be decrypted. Keep your own offline copy of anything you cannot afford to lose.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Face ID / Touch ID — to lock the app itself. In the default mode, opening an entry relies on the app lock, which also accepts your iPhone passcode.
  • Photos — only if you lock photos, to delete the originals you choose.
  • Microphone — only if you record a voice note to your future self; the recording isn’t synced to iCloud, but a device backup includes it.
  • Screen Time (Family Controls) — only if you choose apps to close. The apps you pick are represented by tokens that never leave your device; we never learn which apps they are.
  • Notifications — local reminders when a wait ends. By default they don’t show an entry’s name.

Links you share into the app

When you share a shop link into TimeLocked, the app loads that page directly from your device (without cookies or history) to read the product name and price. The shop sees an ordinary request from your device; we receive nothing.

Analytics we collect

To learn how the app is used, it sends usage events to Google Analytics for Firebase. They are tied to a random app-instance identifier generated by Firebase, not to your name, email or advertising identifier (the app does not use IDFA and does not track you across other apps). What is sent:

  • Actions and screens — for example an entry was created, a wait was requested, cancelled or completed, an entry was revealed or erased, a purchase was started.
  • Coarse attributes — lock length, entry type and category, whether an entry has a name, a letter or a price (never what they say), counts, the length of the text and money not spent in buckets, how long things took, which path you walked in by (an angry message, a purchase or closing apps), whether the app may send notifications or use Screen Time, whether you have Premium, whether the copy came from the App Store or TestFlight, which broad group your onboarding answer falls into (keeping something away from yourself, deciding about something, or none), whether a milestone was reached, whether iCloud is available on the device and how the encryption key is held (iCloud Keychain or a master password), whether sync is on, whether you hold a lock longer than 7 days, how far through a wait you cancelled (under or over half), and why an unlock or a reveal failed (for example “offline”, “Face ID didn’t match”). Your exact onboarding answer and your urge ratings stay with you and are not sent.
  • Purchases — when you buy or renew Premium, Firebase records the purchase event and its price.
  • Ad attribution — if you installed after tapping an Apple Search Ads ad, the campaign identifier, the country or region, and the conversion type.
  • Technical data Firebase records by default — app version, device model, OS version, coarse location (country/region/city derived from IP), and timestamps of each event.

Crash reports. From version 2.8, the app also sends a report to Firebase Crashlytics when the app crashes: where in the code it happened and the system’s error message, device model, OS and app version, the state of the device (such as free memory and storage, and orientation), the analytics events above that led up to it, and random installation identifiers generated by Firebase. The app adds nothing you typed or locked to them. Firebase keeps crash reports and their identifiers for 90 days. A crash is also counted in Google Analytics.

Versions before 2.5 also sent the exact onboarding answer (for example “secrets”, “shopping”, “gambling”, “no contact”), urge ratings (1–3), the hour of day you tapped “close apps in these hours”, which template you previewed, use of the Panic screen, and the ad group and keyword identifiers of an Apple Search Ads ad. Updating the app stops this.

Analytics never include entry names, entry content, letters, voice notes, photos, decrypted data, links, Screen Time app choices, or anything you type into feedback. Google Analytics keeps event-level data for 2 months and user-level data for 14 months from your last activity, and processes it under Firebase’s privacy terms and Google’s Privacy Policy.

Purchases

Premium is sold by Apple through the App Store. We never see your payment details. Subscriptions renew automatically until you cancel them in Settings → your name → Subscriptions on your iPhone, even if the app shows that every feature is open to you.

Your choices

  • Turn iCloud sync off in the app’s Settings; delete the iCloud copies while keeping entries on the device; or reset the vault to erase everything.
  • Deleting the app removes local data. If sync is on, entries remain in your iCloud until you delete them there or reset the vault.
  • Analytics: turn off Settings › Privacy › “Share usage analytics” (available from version 2.5). It covers crash reports too: a crash that happens after you turn it off is never sent. From the next launch the app doesn’t start Firebase and doesn’t exchange the Apple Search Ads attribution token. Data sent before you turned it off stays with Google for the retention periods above. Because analytics are not linked to who you are, we cannot find or delete a specific person’s data on request.

Children

TimeLocked is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Changes

We update this policy when the app changes what it stores or sends; the date above shows the latest version.
